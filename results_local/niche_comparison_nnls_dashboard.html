Niche comparison under the NNLS label transfer

V1 vs V2 · scVI/scGPT annotation × Banksy/STAGATE domains · k=8 · 5 seeds per arm

Regenerated from the NNLS deconvolution transfer. Supersedes the previous dashboard, whose Q1 composition cosines were single-run point estimates later shown to vary by 0.20–0.36 across seeds alone.

Why these numbers moved. The baseline transfer assigned each Leiden cluster one label by argmax correlation to a reference centroid. Cells of the contested EN-IT subtypes never exceeded ~26% of any scGPT cluster, so that argmax could never land on them. NNLS (non-negative least squares) instead fits each cluster mean as a non-negative mixture of all 35 reference centroids, so a type present at 26% surfaces as a 0.26 weight rather than vanishing.

1. Composition must read the mixture, not the hard labels

NNLS still writes one hard label per cell (the mixture's argmax) for downstream compatibility — but in the scGPT arm that argmax still never lands on the contested types. Building composition from hard labels would therefore recover nothing. All composition below is the mean mixture vector per domain.

2. Q1 — does the annotation method change niche composition?

Do not compare the raw cosines across representations. NNLS composition vectors are dense over all 35 types; the baseline's were sparse over each arm's reached labels only (scVI 16–17, scGPT 10–13) with partly disjoint support. Cosine between two dense vectors is systematically higher regardless of whether the arms agree better. The matched−mismatched gap below cancels that and is the figure to cite.
